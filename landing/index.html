The Problem

A wrong order costs more than one dish.

Four pressures on every dinner service, and the technical gap that kept voice ordering from solving them.

Staffing

62%

of restaurant operators call recruiting and retaining staff a significant challenge.[1]

Waiters and waitresses turned over at 131% in 2025, so the floor is always training someone new.

The LanternA voice waiter at every table takes the whole order, so staff can serve instead of writing tickets.

Order Accuracy

1 in 10

drive-thru orders was still wrong in 2026.[2]

Average accuracy in the 2026 Drive-Thru Study was 89.3%. Lanes running AI voice ordering reached 90.8%.

The LanternCode checks every change against the live menu, so a dish, modifier or price can’t be invented.

Allergies

33%

of restaurant servers reported receiving any food-allergy training.[3]

Nearly half of fatal food-allergy reactions over 13 years were caused by food from a restaurant or other food-service establishment.

The LanternAllergies are kept for the whole order, and a conflicting dish is refused before it reaches the kitchen.

Languages

1.52B

international tourist arrivals in 2025, up 4% on 2024.[4]

Each is a guest who may not speak the language the menu is written in.

The LanternGuests order in their own language. Dish names and prices stay exactly as written on the menu.

    How It Works

    The model interprets. Code decides.

    One guest sentence, traced end to end. The same pipeline runs on an edge box in the restaurant or as the hosted demo; only the model and the voice change.

    1. 01

      Hear

      Universal-3.5 Pro, streaming

      16 kHz PCM over one WebSocket. Interim words appear on the table device while the guest is still speaking.

      54 menu keyterms

    2. 02

      Interpret

      Qwen3 4B on Ollama · JSON schema, thinking off 3.5 Flash-Lite · JSON schema

      One call per turn returns an intent and a reference such as offered_all. The model never picks tools, writes the order or words the reply.

      ~1.46k tokens, every turn

    3. 03

      Decide

      WAL mode · deterministic resolver and validator

      Resolves “those two” and “instead”, checks dishes, modifiers and allergens, and writes an immutable revision. Memory lives here, not in the prompt.

      10 / 10 six-turn orders

    4. 04

      Reply

      Template renderer · English and Spanish

      Names, prices and totals are read from the saved revision, so the waiter can’t misquote the menu.

      Verbatim menu

    5. 05

      Speak

      Kokoro-82M, local · 24 kHz streamed PCM Sonic-3 · streamed PCM

      A one-second spoken acknowledgement covers the wait, and the real reply queues right behind it.

      1,752 ms to first reply audio, p50

    6. 06

      Sync

      WebSocket /ws/ops to the Kitchen Display

      Placed orders reach the kitchen; kitchen decisions carry the order’s revision and are spoken back at the table.

      50+ automated tests

    Everything starts with what the guest said.

    Universal-3.5 Pro streaming is tuned for a noisy dining room and a menu full of Vietnamese dish names.

    400 msmin_turn_silence 1,000 msmax_turn_silence

    “We’ll take those two, please.” End of turn at confidence ≥ 0.4, then the transcript is formatted.

    • Keyterms prompting

      54 terms generated from the live menu, so dish names come through intact.

      Bun Bo HueStir-fried Morning GloryPomelo Salad with Shrimpno fish sauceCaramelized Pork in Clay Pot

    • Tuned turn detection

      Short pauses don’t cut a guest off; a finished sentence is answered quickly.

      min_turn_silence=400max_turn_silence=1000end_of_turn_confidence_threshold=0.4format_turns=True

    • Barge-in

      SpeechStarted cancels the reply in progress and stops playback on the device, so guests can talk over a long readback.

    • Resilient connect

      A 5 s handshake timeout with an explicit retry budget. Stalled voice sessions went from 3 / 5 to 0 / 5.
